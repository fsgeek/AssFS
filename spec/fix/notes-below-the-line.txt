Why it matters despite being a non-default option: journal_async_commit
exists to cut fsync latency, by dropping the wait for the data blocks and
the PREFLUSH|FUA commit write in favour of one trailing flush.  That pays
off most where cache flushes are expensive, which mostly means devices
with volatile write caches, which is also where a power cut mid-commit is
most likely to leave the torn state.  Measured on one flush-expensive
stack (QEMU over a Hyper-V guest disk on a consumer NVMe drive, every
fsync a journal commit): async commit gives about +68% fsync throughput
and -40% median latency, one flush per commit instead of two.  On bare
metal (consumer NVMe, WD SN850X, native FUA) it gives +33% throughput and
-23% median latency.  And the dm-log-writes capture below shows the
window comes from jbd2's own submission order, so even devices with
power-loss protection are not exempt.  The people who choose this option
are the ones exposed, and the safety argument it was built on, the v1
whole-transaction checksum, does not apply under csum v2/v3, which
metadata_csum selects.

How this was found and tested, since it was done with an AI assistant
(Claude Opus 5.5, in Claude Code):

- Found by modelling jbd2's commit and recovery paths in TLA+ from the
  7.3-rc7 source, as part of a broader exercise model-checking journal
  recovery designs.  The model predicted the partial replay; TLC finds it
  in a few hundred states.  The models are public (link in the changelog).
- The assistant wrote the patch, the TLA+ models, and the test scripts;
  I reviewed the patch and the reasoning and am responsible for it.
- Also reproduced and tested in QEMU (TCG, tinyconfig + ext4/jbd2) on 7.3-rc7
  with and without the patch, and with e2fsck from e2fsprogs next with
  and without the companion e2fsprogs patch.  Test images are built with
  debugfs ("jo -c; jw; jc", one and two transactions), with one data
  block's journal copy overwritten, and with
  JBD2_FEATURE_INCOMPAT_ASYNC_COMMIT set in the journal superblock:

    image                      7.3-rc7                       patched
    async, T1 torn             1000 replayed, 1001 not;      nothing replayed;
                               mount refused                 mount succeeds
    async, T1 torn, T2 valid   T1 half and T2 replayed;      nothing replayed;
                               mount refused                 "transaction 1 is corrupt"
    async, intact              replayed                      replayed
    sync, torn / intact        refused / replayed            unchanged
    async, 40-block T1, T2,    T1 minus that block and T2    nothing replayed;
      EIO on one T1 block      replayed; mount refused       recovery fails
      (QEMU blkdebug)

- Writer side: captured with dm-log-writes on stock 7.3-rc7 (six fsyncs);
  every prefix of the completion-order log was replayed and checked
  with e2fsck, as xfstests' replay-log tests do.  The six prefixes that
  tear a transaction are recovered cleanly with the patch; without it
  e2fsck partly replays all six, leaving unattached inodes or
  unconnected or corrupted directories in four of them.
  The log, the per-prefix results and the scripts are in the repo linked
  from the changelog.
- Not done: xfstests has not been run.
- Left alone: tid 0 doubles as "unset" for info->end_transaction, as in
  the v1 path; calc_chksums() (v1) turns a read error into an end of log,
  which can drop committed transactions silently -- the same shape this
  patch avoids for v2/v3; and this is a third copy of the tag walk
  (count_tags, jbd2_do_replay).  Happy to follow up on any of these.
- The hoisted j_failed_commit check also changes one existing case: a v2/v3
  async journal whose commit block fails its checksum and is followed by a
  later valid commit used to be truncated silently and is now reported
  as corrupt.  This seemed right by the same argument, but I'd welcome
  a view on it.
